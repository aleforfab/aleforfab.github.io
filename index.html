01 — Flagship study

Vortex generators vs. deep stall

A comparative numerical wind-tunnel study: the same wing profile, twice — clean, then fitted with boundary-layer vortex generators — solved at 9°, 15° and 21° of incidence.

The question

Vortex generators are cheap, passive devices bolted onto wings and wind-turbine blades. Do they actually buy anything before the wing stalls — and how much do they buy as it starts to?

Six steady-state, pressure-based RANS configurations isolate that answer: lift and drag extracted from Fluent force reports, then cross-checked against pathlines, velocity magnitude and turbulent kinetic energy fields.

Method

  1. 01

    Geometry & domain

    Rectangular fluid envelope built around the profile in Ansys Discovery, with shared topology so the wing becomes a conformal internal wall.

  2. 02

    Volumetric mesh

    3D grid generated in Ansys Meshing, refined near the wall where the boundary layer and the generators live.

  3. 03

    Solve & extract

    Steady pressure-based solve in Fluent; lift and drag pulled from force reports, fields exported for pathlines, |V| and k.

What the fields show

09°

Divergence before the numbers notice

The baseline already carries a small leading-edge recirculation. The VG wing stays attached over the entire chord — a qualitative lead that costs almost nothing in measured forces yet.

15°

The transition regime

Both wings now show a comparable leading-edge vortex and near-identical finesse (1.64 vs 1.62). The VG wing is simply one incidence notch late to separate.

21°

Where the device earns its keep

The baseline develops a large main vortex plus a secondary one — deep separation. The VG wing keeps a single contained vortex and gains 8.1% finesse as the baseline begins to drop.

Next steps noted in the report: extend the sweep to 24°–28° to capture the post-stall lift collapse, and run a mesh-independence study with y⁺ resolution around the generators.

Conical pin-fin heat sink
